Check case citations in a brief before filing

For solo attorneys, small law firms, paralegals, and law-school legal-writing courses. Confirm that every case citation in a draft brief points to a findable case before the brief is filed.

Check citations in a draft brief

Not legal advice. This page and the tool are not legal advice. Always read and verify every authority yourself.

The checker only helps locate citations for the attorney's own review. It does not decide whether a case is still good law, has been overruled, or supports an argument.

01

Why check a draft brief before filing

A draft brief can look finished and still cite a case that cannot be found. The risk is higher in an AI-drafted brief: the citation can show a plausible case name, reporter, volume, page, and year, and still match no decision in a reporter. Courts have sanctioned attorneys for briefs citing cases that could not be found. The attorney who signs the brief is responsible for every authority in it. Paralegals and legal-writing courses can run the same check before filing or submission.

Locating a record is only the first step. A citation that cannot be found is not ready to file, and one that can be found still has to be read.

02

Pre-filing checklist
  1. Extract every citation. Walk the brief, including footnotes, and list every case citation and short form. Keep statutes, regulations, and record cites separate. The checker looks up reported court cases and I&N Dec. decisions. It does not look up statutes, regulations, the Federal Register, or AAO decisions.
  2. Confirm the case exists in a reporter or official source. Open the volume and page in a reporter, on the court's site, or in a database the firm already uses.
  3. Confirm the name, reporter, volume, page, and year match the source you opened. A nearby decision, a swapped page, or the wrong year is still the wrong cite.
  4. Read the cited passage yourself. The checker does not read the opinion and does not say whether the case supports the argument.
  5. Check pin cites and quotations by hand. The checker never checks pinpoint pages or quoted sentences.
03

How the /legal checker helps with the first steps

Paste the brief into the citation checker, or upload a plain-text .txt file. Text copied from Word or a PDF can be pasted. The upload control accepts a plain-text file only.

Extraction runs in the browser. The document is not uploaded. For a reported court citation, the page sends only volume, reporter, and page to this site, which asks CourtListener. I&N Dec. citations are checked against the BIA library on this site. A volume that library does not contain is marked Not checked, and the row names the volumes it does have.

Each extracted row is labeled for review. Verified — found means a record was located at that volume and page. The row shows the record's case name and year when the lookup returned them, and it notes when the name in the brief differs. Found means the citation was located. It does not mean the case is still good law, has been overruled, or supports the argument. Not found means no matching record came back. Treat it as a citation that cannot be found until someone locates it in a reporter or official source. A miss in one lookup is not proof that no such case exists. Not checked means the lookup did not run, and the row says why. Unrecognized citation-like passages are listed separately, because the extracted list can be incomplete.

The table shows a standard format built only from extracted fields. It is not a certified Bluebook citation and not a verification result. Each row has a manual-check link. You can copy a Markdown checklist or download JSON. Review marks stay in that browser.

FAQ

Who should use this page?

Solo attorneys, small law firms, paralegals preparing a draft for an attorney, and law-school legal-writing courses. It helps locate citations for that review.

Does "Verified — found" mean the case is safe to rely on?

No. It means a record was located at that volume and page. It does not mean the case is still good law, has not been overruled, or supports the point in the brief. Read the opinion.

What should we do with Not found and Not checked?

Review both by hand before filing. Not found means no matching record came back. Not checked means the lookup did not run, and the row gives the reason. Search a reporter or official source.

Does the brief leave the browser?

Pasted or uploaded text stays in the browser. The document is not uploaded. For a reported court citation, only volume, reporter, and page are sent to this site, which asks CourtListener.
