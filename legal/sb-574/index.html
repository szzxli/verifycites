California SB 574: citation verification for court filings

SB 574 (Umberg) was chaptered as Chapter 858, Statutes of 2026, signed September 30, 2026, and takes effect January 1, 2027. It adds duties for attorneys who use generative AI, a citation rule for court filings, limits for arbitrators, and a Judicial Council review item. Source for every statement on this page: the official bill text on leginfo, linked below.

01 The citation rule

Code of Civil Procedure section 128.7(b)(2)(A), as added by SB 574, section 3, states:

“A brief, pleading, motion, or any other paper filed in any court shall not contain any citations that an attorney responsible for submitting the pleading has not personally verified, including any citation provided by generative artificial intelligence.”

The rule covers every citation in a filed paper, whatever its source. The chaptered text says “personally verified”; earlier drafts added the word “read” before “verified”.

02 Duties for attorneys using generative AI

Business and Professions Code section 6068.1, as added by SB 574, section 1, provides that an attorney shall not delegate the practice of law to generative artificial intelligence, and that an attorney who uses generative artificial intelligence to assist in the practice of law shall: keep confidential, personal identifying, and other nonpublic information out of systems where access is not restricted to the attorney and authorized persons; take reasonable steps to verify the accuracy of generative artificial intelligence outputs, including the accuracy of all case and statutory citations, and correct erroneous output in any material used by the attorney; and disclose the use of generative artificial intelligence to the court for all documents submitted to the court.

03 Arbitrators

Code of Civil Procedure section 1282.1, as added by SB 574, section 5, provides that an arbitrator shall not delegate any part of their decisionmaking process to any generative artificial intelligence tool, and shall not rely on information generated by generative artificial intelligence outside the record without making appropriate disclosures to the parties beforehand and, as far as practical, allowing the parties to comment on its use.

04 Judicial Council review

Code of Civil Procedure section 180, as added by SB 574, section 4, states: “The Judicial Council shall publicly revisit, and revise as necessary, Standard 10.80 of the California Standards of Judicial Administration to incorporate any necessary changes to reflect the further development of generative artificial intelligence.”

05 Notes

The citation rule states the duty without prescribing a verification method or a separate form. This page states facts from the bill text only.
