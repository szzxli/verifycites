Paralegal cite check for a brief before filing

For paralegals and legal assistants at solo practices and small law firms, and for law-school legal-writing courses. Cite-check an attorney's draft brief or motion, then hand the open items back before filing or before the draft is turned in.

Check case citations in the draft

Not legal advice. Neither this page nor the checker is legal advice. The attorney who files still has to read every authority.

What the checker can do is help you locate citations for that reading. It does not tell you whether a case is still good law. It does not tell you whether a case has been overruled. It does not tell you whether a case supports an argument.

01

Who does the cite check, and why it matters

On a brief or motion, the cite check is usually done by a paralegal or legal assistant, then given to the supervising attorney who will file. At a solo practice or a small firm, that list is often the last pass before the attorney signs. A legal-writing class can follow the same order before a student submits a draft.

Do the pass because a citation line can look complete and still fail to match any decision you can open. If you cannot find the decision, stop and put that cite on the open list. If you can find it, the attorney still has to read it. Your job is the list: what was located, what could not be located, and what is left for the attorney. The list does not stand in for that reading.

02

Cite-check workflow before filing

Use this case citation check checklist on the draft the attorney intends to file. The citation checker can help with the extract step and the first locate step for the citation types it covers. The other steps stay with the person doing the cite check.

  1. Gather the draft, including footnotes. Include the table of authorities if the draft has one. Work from the current copy, not an earlier one.
  2. Extract every case citation and every short form. Write down Id., supra, and a party name used with a pinpoint, and note the full citation each short form points to. Statutes, regulations, and cites to the record go on their own list, off the case list. Reported court citations and I&N Dec. citations are in scope. Statutes, regulations, Federal Register cites, and AAO decisions are left unchecked.
  3. Locate each case in a reporter or an official source. Go to that volume and page in a reporter, on the site of the court that decided it, or in the research service the office already subscribes to.
  4. Match the name, reporter, volume, page, and year against the source in front of you. The next page over, a swapped volume, or a year taken from a different decision is not the same cite.
  5. Flag pin cites and quotations for the attorney to check by hand. Finding the case does not confirm a quoted sentence or a pinpoint page. The checker never reads the opinion. Pinpoint pages and quoted sentences are outside what it checks.
  6. Hand back a list of open items. Include citations that could not be located, rows the checker did not check, short forms you could not tie to a full citation, and every pin cite and quotation for the attorney.
03

How the checker helps with extraction and locate

Open the citation checker and paste the draft, or upload a .txt file. If the draft lives in Word or in a PDF, copy the words out and paste them. The file control takes plain text and will not accept those other formats.

The extract step runs on this computer, in the browser. The draft is not sent as a file. A reported court citation contributes three fields only: volume, reporter, and page. The lookup service receives those fields and asks CourtListener. For an I&N Dec. cite, the comparison uses the BIA library that ships with this site. If the cited volume is not in that library, the row is labeled Not checked and the row lists the volumes the library covers. An I&N Dec. short form written with "at" and no starting page is also labeled Not checked, because that match needs the decision's starting page.

Each extracted row gets a label you can hand back. The row reads Verified — found after the lookup locates a record at that volume and page. If the lookup returned a case name and a year, the row shows them. When the name in the draft does not match that record, the row says so. The label means the cite was located. It does not mean the case is still good law. It does not mean the case has not been overruled. It does not mean the case supports an argument. Not found is the label when the lookup returned no matching record. Keep the row open until a person locates the decision in a reporter or an official source. Nothing in one empty result proves that no such decision exists, so treat the cite as one that could not be located. Not checked is the label when the lookup did not run. Read the reason on the row. Passages that resemble citations but were not extracted are shown in their own list. Neither that list nor the table is a promise that every cite in the draft was caught.

Id. and supra are not volume-reporter-page cites, and the checker does not look them up. They stay on the hand list from the workflow. A pinpoint shown on a row is the pinpoint copied from the draft. The opinion is not opened to test it.

Beside the label, the table prints a standard format made only from pieces the extractor took from the draft. That format is not a certified Bluebook cite, and the lookup did not produce it. Each row includes a link you can open while you check the cite yourself. Copy the Markdown checklist, or download the JSON file, when you are ready to send the work on. Any review marks you set remain in that browser.

04

What to hand back to the supervising attorney

Give the attorney enough to finish the review without redoing the list. Include:

  • The draft you checked, with footnotes.
  • The Markdown checklist or the JSON download from the checker.
  • Every Not found row and every Not checked row, including the reason on the row.
  • The separate list of passages that were not extracted.
  • Short forms you tied to a full citation, and any short form you could not tie.
  • Pin cites and quotations, marked for the attorney to read.
  • Any row where the name in the draft differs from the record, and any reporter, volume, page, or year that did not match the source you opened.

On the note, say that Verified — found only records that a decision was located. The checker does not tell the attorney whether a case is still good law. It does not tell the attorney whether a case has been overruled. It does not tell the attorney whether a case supports an argument. The attorney reads the opinion and chooses what to file.

A separate page covers the brief citation check an attorney may run before filing. On an immigration draft, the immigration page explains the route for each immigration citation type. I&N Dec. cites in that work are compared with the BIA library kept here.

FAQ

Who should run this cite check?

Paralegals and legal assistants at solo practices and small firms, before an attorney files a brief or motion. A law-school legal-writing course can use the same checklist before a draft is submitted. The attorney still reads every authority.

How do I cite check a brief before filing?

Gather the current draft, including footnotes. List every case citation and short form. Use the checker to extract the citations it recognizes and to locate reported court citations and I&N Dec. citations. Match the name, reporter, volume, page, and year to a reporter or an official source. Flag pin cites and quotations. Hand the open items to the attorney.

Does "Verified — found" mean the citation is ready to file?

No. The only fact in that label is that a record was located at that volume and page. That fact does not mean the case is still good law. It does not mean the case has not been overruled. It does not mean the case supports the draft's point. The attorney has to read the opinion. Pin cites and quotations are still waiting for a hand check.

What should I send back for Not found and Not checked?

Send both. Not found means the lookup returned no record, so look in a reporter or an official source before anyone calls the cite one that cannot be found. Not checked means this run did not perform the lookup; the reason is written on the row. If the reason is an I&N Dec. volume missing from the BIA library, the row also lists the volumes that library holds.

Is the draft uploaded?

No. Words you paste, and the text of a .txt file you upload, remain on this computer. The draft is not sent as a document. From a reported court citation, the lookup service receives the volume, the reporter, and the page, then asks CourtListener. I&N Dec. cites are compared with the BIA library stored on this site.

What about a brief that also cites immigration authorities?

Reported court cites in that draft follow the same locate step. I&N Dec. cites are compared with the BIA library stored on this site. Statutes, regulations, Federal Register cites, and AAO decisions stay unchecked. The immigration page describes how those citation types are sorted, and the brief citation check page is the attorney's pre-filing review.
