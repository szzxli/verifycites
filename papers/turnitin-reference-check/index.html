Does Turnitin check whether your references exist?

Short answer: no. Here is what Turnitin does check, how to check a reference list by hand, and a free tool that speeds up the lookups.

Open the citation checker
§1

The short answer

Turnitin compares the text of your paper with its database. It does not look up the sources in your reference list.

  • The Similarity Report highlights text in your paper that matches text in Turnitin's database of web pages, past student papers and published journals. A reference to a paper that can't be found anywhere is still original text, so it can come through with no match at all.
  • The Citations Check in Draft Coach looks for matching pairs of citations and references: an in-text citation with no reference, or a reference that is never cited. Turnitin's guide lists MLA and APA as the styles it detects. It does not check whether the work behind a reference exists.

Aalto University's Turnitin page says it plainly: “Turnitin does not check the authenticity of references in the work, regardless of whether they are human-written or AI-generated.”

So a reference list can pass Turnitin looking tidy while one entry points to a paper that can't be found, carries a DOI that opens a different paper, or has the wrong authors or year. (Sources checked November 2026.)

§2

How to check your references by hand

This works for any reference list. It is slow for a long one, but you should know how to do it.

  1. Search the exact title in quotes in Google Scholar or your library's search. If nothing with that title turns up, treat the entry as unverifiable until you find it.
  2. Open the DOI. If the entry has one, go to https://doi.org/ followed by the DOI. Make sure it lands on the same paper, with the same title and authors, not on a different one.
  3. Compare title, authors and year with the publisher's page or an index record. A year off by one is often the preprint versus the published version. A different title or first author usually means the entry is wrong.
  4. Read the passage you rely on. Open the source and find where it says what your sentence says. A real paper can still be the wrong citation.
  5. Ask a librarian about anything you still can't find. Don't keep an entry just because it looks right.
§3

Where /papers helps

The free /papers checker does the lookups in steps 1 to 3 for every reference at once. Step 4 is still yours.

Paste a reference list or a draft (markdown or plain text), upload a .docx or .pdf, or paste or upload a BibTeX .bib file. For each citation, the checker:

  • looks up whether it can be found as an existing record in public registries (Crossref, DataCite/arXiv, OpenAlex, Europe PMC, DBLP; Semantic Scholar as a last resort);
  • checks whether a DOI or other identifier in the entry resolves and matches that record;
  • when a record is found, compares the title, authors and year with it. If the title or the authors don't match, or the year differs by more than one, the result is Found, but details don't match, not a verified citation. A year off by one stays found and is labeled as a likely preprint versus published difference. A field missing from the reference or the record is labeled Not compared, with the reason;
  • flags papers that have been retracted.

Your draft or file stays in your browser. Only citation identifiers and reference text are sent to the registries.

§4

What /papers does not check

  • It does not compare the venue, volume, issue or pages. A reference with the right title, authors and year but the wrong journal or page range will still show as found.
  • The author comparison uses surnames only: the first author, and the overlap between the two lists. A list that only partly overlaps is reported as partial, not as a mismatch, and first names and initials are not compared.
  • It does not check whether a source supports your sentence. Finding a record, or a DOI that resolves, does not mean the paper says what you cite it for. If you paste a full draft, an optional screen can compare a claim with the cited paper's abstract only. It does not read the full text.
  • It is not a style checker or a similarity checker. It does not check APA or MLA formatting, and it does not compare your text with other documents.
  • Web pages, software, datasets and standards usually have no scholarly record, so the checker cannot confirm them. Check those by hand.

“Not found” is not proof that a work does not exist. A real paper can be missing from the free indexes. Use the results to decide which references to open and check yourself.
